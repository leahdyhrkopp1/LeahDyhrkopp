Case study · Data Visualization

Graduate Admissions Executive Dashboard

For my data visualization final, I took a raw admissions dataset I had never seen before, cleaned and prepared it in Alteryx, and built a one-page Tableau dashboard that answers three questions a program director would ask: who is applying, how far do they get, and what do graduates earn?

Course
Data Visualization, M.S. AI for Business
Tools
Alteryx · Tableau
Data
3,000+ applicant records from 59 countries
When
October 2026
The brief

The final gave us a new dataset with no warning about what it would contain: thousands of applicant records with each person’s status, country of origin, test scores and, for graduates, where they were hired and what they were paid. The task was to turn that into something leadership could read in under a minute.

I designed for one reader, a program director deciding where to recruit and how to talk about outcomes, and built every view to answer one of their questions.

How I built it

  1. Prepare in Alteryx

    Built a workflow to clean and organize the raw Excel data into a single analysis-ready table for Tableau.

  2. Model in Tableau

    Counted unique applicants with COUNTD([Student ID]), grouped final decisions into an “Applied” bucket, and added a parameter that switches salary between mean and median.

  3. Design the dashboard

    Combined a map of origin countries, an applicant-status chart and salary by industry with an average reference line, and connected them with a filter action so clicking a country updates everything.

What it shows
  • 51%

    One country dominates the pipeline. Of applicants with a recorded country, 1,436 came from China, more than India (589) and the U.S. (497) combined. The program’s outcomes depend heavily on one recruiting market.

  • 17.5%

    Few final decisions end in enrollment. Of 1,673 applicants who reached a final outcome, 293 enrolled. About half were denied, and 519 (31%) withdrew, which suggests the program is losing admitted students.

  • $71K

    Graduates land solid salaries. Across 381 placements, the average starting salary was $71,385 (median $70,000). Consulting hired the most graduates (131) at about $70K, and financial services paid the most among industries with 20+ hires, at about $77K.

  • n = 4

    Why the median toggle matters. Marketing shows the highest average salary ($85K), but it comes from only four people. Switching to the median and flagging small samples keeps a few outliers from distorting the story.

What I took away

A dashboard is only useful if its reader trusts it. Small choices like counting unique applicants, offering a median next to the mean and showing how many people stand behind each bar are what separate a chart that looks good from one you can make decisions with.
