Graduate Admissions Executive Dashboard

For my data visualization final, I was handed a raw admissions dataset I had never seen before. I cleaned it up in Alteryx and built a one-page Tableau dashboard that answers the three questions a program director would ask first: Who is applying? How far do they get? What do graduates earn?

Course
Data Visualization and Communication (ITOM 6212)
Tools
Alteryx · Tableau
Data
3,000+ applicants from 59 countries
When
October 2026
The assignment

The final gave us a brand-new dataset with no preview of what was inside: thousands of applicant records with each person’s status, country of origin and test scores, plus where graduates were hired and what they were paid. Our job was to turn all of that into something leadership could understand in under a minute.

I pictured one reader, a program director deciding where to recruit and how to talk about outcomes, and made sure every chart answered one of their questions.

What I did

  1. Prepared the data in Alteryx

    I built a workflow to clean and organize the raw Excel data into one table that was ready for Tableau.

  2. Modeled it in Tableau

    I counted unique applicants with COUNTD([Student ID]), grouped final decisions together and added a switch between mean and median salary.

  3. Designed the dashboard

    I combined a map of applicants’ home countries, an applicant status chart and salary by industry, then linked them so clicking a country filters everything.

What I found
  • 51%

    One country dominates the pipeline. Of the applicants with a recorded country, 1,436 came from China, more than India (589) and the U.S. (497) combined. That means the program’s results depend heavily on a single recruiting market.

  • 17.5%

    Few final decisions end in enrollment. Of the 1,673 applicants who reached a final outcome, 293 enrolled. About half were denied, and 519 (31%) withdrew, which is a lot of interest the program loses along the way.

  • $71K

    Graduates land solid salaries. Across 381 placements, the average starting salary was $71,385 (median $70,000). Consulting hired the most graduates (131) at about $70K, and financial services paid the most among industries with 20+ hires, at about $77K.

  • n = 4

    Averages can mislead. Marketing shows the highest average salary ($85K), but that number comes from just four people. Offering the median and flagging small samples keeps a few outliers from skewing the story.

What I learned

A dashboard only helps if the person reading it trusts it. Small choices, like counting unique applicants, offering a median next to the mean and showing how many people stand behind each bar, are what turn a nice-looking chart into one you can actually make decisions with.

Working with a dataset I’d never seen, on a clock, also taught me to slow down at the start. Knowing exactly what each column meant before building anything saved me from charts that looked right but weren’t.
